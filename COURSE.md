# HCI Methods Studio

Binyan Xu · Creative technology, embodied interaction, and health

Proposed 12-week course. See the website for teaching practice, assessment, and draft policies.

## Weekly sequence

### Week 1: A question worth asking

Framing & positionality

Turn a creative or health interest into a researchable question.

**Studio:** Map whose experience matters, competing explanations, and evidence that could distinguish them.

**Deliverable:** Question card + positionality memo

**Readings:** icap

### Week 2: Read the argument

Reading research

Separate the question, evidence, method, and contribution.

**Studio:** Reconstruct a paper’s evidence chain. Identify one claim the evidence cannot establish.

**Deliverable:** Annotated evidence map

**Readings:** rtd

### Week 3: Listen before you build

Interviews & observation

Understand lived experience and context without assuming a solution.

**Studio:** Practice a consent-based interview about an everyday interaction. Distinguish observation from interpretation.

**Deliverable:** Interview guide + fieldnote

**Readings:** theme

### Week 4: Make sense of experience

Thematic analysis

Develop interpretations grounded in material and reflexive decisions.

**Studio:** Code shared anonymized practice data, compare interpretations, and maintain a decision trail.

**Deliverable:** Theme map + reflexive memo

**Readings:** theme

### Week 5: Design with, not just for

Participatory design

Examine participation, expertise, and decision-making power.

**Studio:** Plan a co-design activity for a rehabilitation game. Specify which decisions participants control.

**Deliverable:** Co-design plan + power map

**Readings:** codesign, rehab

### Week 6: Build to think

Research through design

Use artifacts to investigate possibilities and generate design knowledge.

**Studio:** Make two competing bodily interaction prototypes; document what each lets you learn.

**Deliverable:** Two prototypes + design rationale

**Readings:** rtd, dance

### Week 7: Test an assumption

Prototyping & think-aloud

Observe use, identify friction, and revise a specific design assumption.

**Studio:** Run a paper-prototype walkthrough with neutral prompts. Use nonclinical scenarios.

**Deliverable:** Usability memo + revision

**Readings:** prototype

### Week 8: Bodies are not sensors

Embodied & multimodal methods

Connect physical interaction to comfort, agency, context, and measurement.

**Studio:** Map calibration, fatigue, sensory experience, and individual differences in a wearable or XR concept.

**Deliverable:** Measurement + experience map

**Readings:** gaze, perception

### Week 9: Compare with care

Experimental design

Plan conditions, outcomes, ordering, and plausible confounds.

**Studio:** Design a comparison of input techniques. Specify a primary outcome and counterbalancing.

**Deliverable:** Study protocol + analysis plan

**Readings:** fitts, gaze

### Week 10: Evidence, not just numbers

Quantitative reasoning

Interpret effects, uncertainty, and limits rather than only significance.

**Studio:** Plot practice data, inspect distributions, and explain alternative interpretations. Discuss analysis assumptions.

**Deliverable:** Results figure + limitations

**Readings:** art

### Week 11: What counts as better?

Health & mixed evidence

Distinguish usability, engagement, feasibility, and clinical benefit.

**Studio:** Combine an experience account and a performance measure. Identify what evidence would still be needed for a health claim.

**Deliverable:** Evidence integration + claim boundaries

**Readings:** rehab, xr

### Week 12: Make the research legible

Proposal & studio critique

Connect the question, methods, analysis, ethics, and contribution.

**Studio:** Present a research proposal. Receive a substantive peer challenge, then revise and write a response.

**Deliverable:** Proposal + response letter

**Readings:** rtd, icap

## Reading room

### rtd: Research Through Design as a Method for Interaction Design Research in HCI

Zimmerman, Forlizzi & Evenson · 2007 · Design

https://jodiforlizzi.phd.sh/publications/467-research-through-design-as-a-method-for-interaction-design-research-in-hci

**Question:** What knowledge does making produce? Which design decisions can another researcher inspect?

**Practice:** Compare two embodied prototypes. Document decisions, alternatives, and transferable insights.

### theme: Using thematic analysis in psychology

Braun & Clarke · 2006 · Qualitative

https://doi.org/10.1191/1478088706qp063oa

**Question:** How do themes answer a question rather than simply sort interview excerpts?

**Practice:** Code a shared anonymized transcript, then compare how researcher perspectives shape interpretation.

### codesign: Co-creation and the new landscapes of design

Sanders & Stappers · 2008 · Design

https://doi.org/10.1080/15710880701875068

**Question:** What decisions can participants actually influence, and at which stages?

**Practice:** Plan a wearable co-design session where participants can redefine both the task and success criteria.

### prototype: Prototyping for tiny fingers

Rettig · 1994 · Design

https://doi.org/10.1145/175276.175288

**Question:** How can a low-fidelity prototype expose assumptions before implementation?

**Practice:** Use paper and role-play to test a gaze interaction before writing code.

### fitts: Fitts’ Law as a Research and Design Tool in Human–Computer Interaction

MacKenzie · 1992 · Quantitative

https://www.yorku.ca/mack/hci1992.html

**Question:** When does the model apply, and how do speed, accuracy, and target geometry relate?

**Practice:** Plan a pointing task, compare input techniques, and document errors and device limitations.

### gaze: A Fitts’ Law Study of Click and Dwell Interaction by Gaze, Head and Mouse with a Head-Mounted Display

Hansen, Rajanna, MacKenzie & Bækgaard · 2018 · Quantitative

https://www.yorku.ca/mack/etra2018.html

**Question:** How are gaze interactions compared? What changes between dwell and click selection?

**Practice:** Map the independent variables, outcomes, ordering, and potential confounds.

### art: The Aligned Rank Transform for Nonparametric Factorial Analyses Using Only ANOVA Procedures

Wobbrock, Findlater, Gergle & Higgins · 2011 · Quantitative

https://faculty.washington.edu/wobbrock/pubs/chi-11.06.pdf

**Question:** Which assumptions does an analysis require, and when should you seek statistical support?

**Practice:** Translate a question into factors and interactions. Explain what must be checked before using ART.

### icap: The ICAP Framework: Linking Cognitive Engagement to Active Learning Outcomes

Chi & Wylie · 2014 · Teaching

https://education.asu.edu/lcl/publications/chi-m-t-h-wylie-r-2014-icap-framework-linking-cognitive-engagement-active-learning

**Question:** How can learners generate explanations and challenge one another rather than only follow instructions?

**Practice:** Redesign a short lecture as individual explanation, peer comparison, and joint revision.

### rehab: “It Depends”: Re-Authoring Play Through Clinical Reasoning in Wearable AR Rehab Games

Xu, Wu, Kweon, Harteveld & Chukoskie · 2026 · Personal research

https://doi.org/10.1145/3772318.3790457

**Question:** How might clinical reasoning complicate fixed movement rules in a rehabilitation game?

**Practice:** Reading prompt: map whose judgments shape the rules. Check the full paper before making claims about its methods or results.

### dance: Dan-sco: An Embodied Performative Interface for Improvisational Co-Creation

Xu, Wu, Chen, Wang & Harteveld · 2026 · Personal research

https://doi.org/10.1145/3803784.3809271

**Question:** What might improvisational co-creation require us to study beyond task completion?

**Practice:** Reading prompt: propose an observation strategy for bodily expression and creative agency.

### perception: Perception VR Studio: Tangible and Soft Interfaces for Embodied Cognition Exploring Material Cognition through Air, Heat, Softness, and Scent in VR Environments

Wu, Xu, Nasri & Harteveld · 2026 · Personal research

https://doi.org/10.1145/3731459.3779052

**Question:** How could we investigate multisensory experience without reducing it to a single score?

**Practice:** Reading prompt: contrast an experiential account with a controlled comparison.

### xr: Co-Designing a Multimodal AR Assistant for Interventional Radiology Workflows

Xu & Wu · 2026 · Personal research

https://doi.org/10.1145/3772363.3799005

**Question:** How could workflow expertise inform the design of an AR assistant?

**Practice:** Reading prompt: identify stakeholders, workflow constraints, and questions for the full paper.
