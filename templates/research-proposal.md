## 1. Question and motivation
A concrete question; whose experience matters; stakes; researcher positionality.

## 2. Related work and gap
Synthesize sources around the question. Explain the gap rather than listing papers.

## 3. Proposed contribution
What could others learn? Distinguish design knowledge, experience accounts, technical contributions, and evaluation evidence.

## 4. Method and rationale
Approach, setting, recruitment if applicable, materials, procedure, comparison or iteration, and alternatives considered. Explain method fit.

## 5. Evidence and analysis
What will be collected? How will it be analyzed? For quantitative work, identify primary outcomes, sample-size rationale, assumptions, and uncertainty. For qualitative work, describe interpretation, reflexivity, and the decision trail. For RtD, document design iterations and knowledge claims.

## 6. Ethics, access, and data governance
Consent and withdrawal, accessibility options, burden, data minimization, storage, access, retention, and relevant institutional review. Do not recruit or collect clinical data before required approvals.

## 7. Feasibility and timeline
Milestones, resources, dependencies, and a smaller viable scope.

## 8. Claim boundaries
What will the study not establish? Distinguish usability and feasibility from clinical benefit. Name plausible alternative explanations.

## 9. Response to critique
State a substantive challenge, the change made, and any unresolved disagreement.

## References and appendices
Protocol, instruments, design log, and AI/tool disclosure where relevant. Do not include sensitive participant records.
