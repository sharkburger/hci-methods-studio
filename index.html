01 / THE COURSE

A proposed 12-week sequence · adaptable by design

From a promising idea
to a defensible study.

Creative practice gives us possibilities. Research methods help us examine what those possibilities mean, for whom, and in which contexts.

This independent course connects a studio approach to HCI inquiry: qualitative interpretation, participatory design, research through design, and empirical evaluation. Health is a recurring context for asking careful questions about agency, access, and evidence.

Designed by Binyan Xu ↗. In dialogue with the creative technology tradition of NYU ITP/IMA. This is an independent teaching resource.

05 / TEACHING PRACTICE

Learning is something we do together

A studio, a seminar,
a community of inquiry.

Read → explain → try → critique → revise.

Sessions alternate close reading, student-led discussion, small method trials, and critique. Students produce explanations and revise them with peers. This design is informed by the ICAP framework; it is a teaching choice, not a guarantee of outcomes.

BEFORE / 30–60 MIN

Bring a question.

Read one core paper. Write its central claim, one piece of evidence, one limitation, and a question that invites disagreement.

TOGETHER / 150 MIN

Think in public.

15 min opening reflection · 30 min discussion · 10 min break · 50 min method studio · 30 min critique · 15 min exit memo.

AFTER / DOCUMENT

Leave a trail.

Keep a research notebook with artifacts, decisions, contradictory evidence, and the next question. Revise one idea after peer feedback.

Assignments & proposed assessment +

Assessment is a draft model for adaptation before a course runs.

  • Research notebook — 20%. Weekly evidence maps, fieldnotes, prototype records, and reflective memos.
  • Reading facilitation — 15%. Lead a discussion that tests an argument rather than reciting the paper.
  • Method sprint — 25%. A small practice study: question, method, material, interpretation, and limitations. A classroom pilot does not establish clinical benefit.
  • Final research proposal — 40%. Question and related work (20% of rubric), method fit (25%), analysis plan (20%), ethics and feasibility (20%), contribution and response to critique (15%).

Final submission: a 2,500–3,500 word proposal plus references, a study protocol or artifact appendix, and a one-page response to peer critique. Timing, accommodations, and submission channels should be set for each offering.

Care, access & research ethics +

Offer alternatives to movement, sensory, and public speaking activities. Ask about access requirements without requiring disclosure of a diagnosis. Participants may pause or stop an activity.

Use synthetic or appropriately consented, de-identified materials for classroom practice. Any study involving patients, clinical settings, or identifiable health data must follow the relevant institutional review and data governance processes before recruitment or collection. Usability, engagement, and clinical efficacy are different claims.

Do not publish participant records in this open repository. Public project write-ups should use cleared materials and state their limitations.

AI use & scholarly responsibility +

Proposed policy: AI may support brainstorming, code troubleshooting, or language editing when its use is disclosed. Students remain responsible for checking claims, sources, code, and interpretations. Reading notes and evidence-based arguments must demonstrate engagement with the source.

Do not upload participant data or confidential research to external AI tools. Include a short disclosure describing the tool, purpose, and what you verified. The instructor should finalize this policy before the course runs.
