01 / THE COURSE

Research foundations + creative technology + health

From a promising idea
to a defensible study.

Creative practice gives us possibilities. Research methods help us examine what those possibilities mean, for whom, and in which contexts.

Explore qualitative inquiry, participatory design, research through making, and empirical evaluation. Each method offers a different way to understand people, examine an interaction, and develop a research contribution. Studio exercises connect these approaches to creative technology, embodied experience, and health.

Designed by Binyan Xu ↗. In dialogue with the creative technology tradition of NYU ITP/IMA. This is an independent teaching resource.

06 / TEACHING PRACTICE

Learning is something we do together

A studio, a seminar,
a community of inquiry.

Read → explain → try → critique → revise.

Sessions alternate close reading, student-led discussion, small method trials, and critique. Students produce explanations and revise them with peers. This design is informed by the ICAP framework; it is a teaching choice, not a guarantee of outcomes.

BEFORE / 30–60 MIN

Bring a question.

Read the assigned material before discussion. Source weeks distinguish READ, STRETCH, and, where present, DO. Map an argument and bring a question for your peers.

TOGETHER / 150 MIN

Think in public.

15 min opening reflection · 30 min discussion · 10 min break · 50 min method studio · 30 min critique · 15 min exit memo.

AFTER / DOCUMENT

Leave a trail.

Keep a research notebook with artifacts, decisions, contradictory evidence, and the next question. Revise one idea after peer feedback.

Reference assessment & project pathway +

The reference model weights participation 10%, reading facilitation 20%, group projects 20%, a mini-project 10%, and an individual research proposal 40%.

Its project pathway moves from an annotated bibliography to a group literature review, then a group proposal, alongside a faculty-guided mini-project and an individual proposal. The literature review adds 7–9 publications; the mini-project spans approximately 5–7 weeks. See each week for the reference milestones.

For this independent studio, specify local meeting dates, submission routes, assessment criteria, and access arrangements before teaching. The templates below are newly authored teaching aids.

Care, access & research ethics +

Offer alternatives to movement, sensory, and public speaking activities. Ask about access requirements without requiring disclosure of a diagnosis. Participants may pause or stop an activity.

Use synthetic or appropriately consented, de-identified materials for classroom practice. Any study involving patients, clinical settings, or identifiable health data must follow the relevant institutional review and data governance processes before recruitment or collection. Usability, engagement, and clinical efficacy are different claims.

Do not publish participant records in this open repository. Public project write-ups should use cleared materials and state their limitations.

Reference AI, attendance & late-work policies +

In the reference course, generative AI is prohibited for reading notes, presentations, and written assignments. Its attendance policy allows two unexplained absences; additional absences affect the grade. Late submissions require exceptional advance arrangements.

These are source-course policies, not a published offering at this site. A future instructor should specify the applicable policies before a course begins. Keep independent reading and original argumentation central, and never upload confidential participant material to external services.
